# Faith in the present, memory in the martyr: Catholic education and the figure of Stepinac as sites of memory in Croatian digital media (2021 to 2025)

**Working paper, version 0.1 (preliminary, before hand validation).** 6 July 2026. DigiKat, Hrvatsko katoličko
sveučilište. This is a first internal draft. Every figure in it is computed from the corpus, as the methods and
results sections explain, and every share should be read as indicative until the coding check described in the
limitations. The reference list is a working set that still needs verification through a dedicated literature review.

## Abstract

Digital media have become a main arena in which religious communities remember. This paper asks whether Catholic
education, the domain the Croatian Catholic Church treats as its chief site for handing on faith, values and
identity, is presented in digital media as a site of collective memory or as a live argument about present values.
We take Pierre Nora's idea of the site of memory and turn it into something we can measure, a construct of four
signals. An anchor counts as a site of memory to the degree that it recurs often, peaks on a commemorative calendar,
carries emotional weight, and is genuinely tied to a remembered past. We apply this to a corpus of about 710,000
posts about religion in Croatian and Bosnian gathered from 2021 to 2026, and we work within an education strand of
176,312 posts covering 2021 to 2025. We find that the institutions and practices of Catholic education, meaning
religious instruction in schools, Catholic schools, the teaching orders, and talk of upbringing and values, are
handled overwhelmingly in the present tense. They recur a great deal, yet they are only weakly tied to the past,
they show no commemorative rhythm, and they carry a register of trust. The single figure of Blessed Alojzije
Stepinac behaves differently. On the signals that tell a site of memory apart, meaning genuine anchoring to the
past, a commemorative peak and a supporting emotional register, he behaves like one. He has by far the strongest
genuine tie to the past, a peak that returns each February around his death anniversary, and an emotional register
of conflict and persecution. Most tellingly, his audience reaches well beyond the Church, since secular and
nationally minded outlets carry him more than they carry any other anchor, even though Catholic sources remain the
majority. In this media space the work of memory travels
through the martyr, not through the school. We offer this screen, built from four signals, as a tool others can reuse, and we
report a method result of its own interest. About seven in ten of the document level ties between religion and the
past turn out to be incidental, so simply counting how often religion and the past show up in the same
post overstates real memorial engagement roughly threefold.

## 1. Uvod / Introduction

Collective memory no longer passes mainly through monuments, textbooks and public ceremonies. It is made and fought
over in digital media (Hoskins 2011 and van Dijck 2007). For religious communities the change matters. The Church's
old instruments for handing on memory, meaning catechesis, schooling and liturgy, now compete for meaning inside a
media space it does not control, one in which secular voices dominate and content circulates by platform logic
rather than pastoral intent (Hjarvard 2008 and Hoover 2006).

Croatia is a good place to look. Catholicism is bound up with national identity, the twentieth century left a
contested memory of the communist state's treatment of the Church, and Catholic education, meaning religious
instruction in state schools, Catholic schools, and the moral formation of the young, returns again and again as a
line of public dispute. The Church itself names education as the place where faith, memory and values are passed on.
This paper asks a plain but open question. In Croatian digital media, is Catholic education actually presented as a
site where memory is handed on, or as a live argument about present values. And if some Catholic anchors do work as
sites of memory, which ones, and who keeps them alive.

We answer by taking an idea from memory theory, Nora's site of memory, turning it into a measure we can run across a
large corpus, and then reading the result back through the theory. The main finding is a clean split. The
institutions of Catholic education are remembered in the present tense. The memory work is done instead by a figure,
the martyr cardinal Alojzije Stepinac, who functions as a shared national site of memory across Catholic and
secular media alike.

## 2. Motivacija i doprinos / Motivation and contribution

Three gaps motivate the study. First, memory studies has thought richly about digital and connective memory but has
rarely measured, at national scale, which candidate anchors actually behave like sites of memory rather than merely
prominent current affairs. Second, work on digital religion has mapped how religion is shaped by media but has
seldom asked what memory work particular religious symbols perform across a whole national media space. Third, and
on method, studies of religion and some topic in large text corpora routinely count overlap and treat it as
engagement. As we show, that move overstates genuine memorial linkage by about three times in this corpus.

The contribution follows from these gaps. Substantively, this is the first computational map, as far as we know for
Croatian media, of which Catholic anchors work as sites of memory, with education as the test case. On method, it
offers a reusable way of measuring the site of memory, built from four signals, checked against a known example and
a known counter example. And it breaks the audience down, showing who produces the memory anchor and who produces
the live argument of today, which is the part that neither a close reading nor a single outlet study could deliver.

## 3. Teorijski okvir i srodni radovi / Theoretical framework and related work (brief)

We lean on three classic anchors and one recent turn, and we keep the review short. Halbwachs (1925, 1950) held that
memory is socially framed, so that remembering always happens within and for groups. This lets us treat the
question of who remembers, the breakdown of the audience below, as part of the finding rather than a detail. Nora
(1989) described sites of memory as the crystallised points, meaning figures, symbols, dates and institutions, to
which a group's memory attaches once living everyday memory has receded. For us the important part is that a genuine
site is durable, it returns as ritual, it carries emotional weight, and it binds a present identity to a remembered
past. Those four properties are exactly what we measure. Nora also stressed that a site of memory becomes national
property, argued over well beyond the community that first held it, which speaks directly to Stepinac's shared
Catholic and secular audience. Jan Assmann (1995), with Aleida Assmann, distinguished communicative memory, which is
lived, informal and bounded by living generations, from cultural memory, which is institutional, canonical and
carried by figures and rites. Education sits on the hinge between the two, and our question is whether the media
treat it as the handing on of cultural memory or as something argued in the here and now. The connective turn (Hoskins
2011 and van Dijck 2007) and the mediatisation of religion (Hjarvard 2008 and Hoover 2006) together explain why the
whole media space, and not the Church's own channels, is the right place to ask how Catholic memory is passed on.
The fourth body this paper must engage, Croatian scholarship on Catholic media and on the memory of Stepinac, is
deliberately left to the dedicated literature review and is not yet worked in here.

## 4. Hipoteze / Hypotheses

This is a descriptive and interpretive study, not a study that tries to prove cause. The hypotheses are directional
expectations that the corpus can bear out or contradict. The first was set before the analysis, in the study
proposal. The second and third are refinements the first analysis suggested, and we offer them as propositions to be
confirmed on fresh data and through the coding check.

The first expectation, set in advance, is that within Catholic education discourse genuine memorial anchoring
concentrates in symbolic figures rather than in educational institutions or practices, so that religious
instruction, Catholic schools and talk of values are handled as a live argument about the values and politics of today with
little tie to the past. A contrary result was genuinely possible, since the institutions could have been heavily
tied to the communist era rupture. The second expectation is that the leading figure, Stepinac, works as a shared
national site of memory, carried by Catholic and by secular or nationally minded media rather than as a devotion
internal to the Church. The third and weaker expectation is that the memory anchor carries an emotional register of
conflict and persecution, meaning anger, fear and sadness, that differs from the register of trust around
institutional discourse.

## 5. Podaci i metode / Data and methods

The DigiKat master corpus holds about 710,307 posts described by 47 variables, in Croatian and Bosnian, from 2021 to
2026, drawn from web portals, which dominate, together with YouTube, Facebook, Twitter or X, Reddit, forums and
comment streams. A post enters the corpus only when it matches at least two distinct Catholic religious terms, using
a lexicon of 95 terms written to catch Croatian inflection. The corpus therefore holds posts about religion from
across the whole media space, and it is dominated by secular outlets rather than being a Catholic archive, which is
a strength, since it lets us watch how the entire media space handles Catholic memory. One collection artefact
shapes all of the timing work. The corpus was gathered in two streams that do not overlap in time. One runs from
2021 to 2024 and the other from 2024 to 2026, and they hand over in 2024. Because of this, raw yearly volume is not
comparable across the handover, and all peaking analysis is run within a single stream.

On top of the corpus wide filter we apply a study specific probe for eight education related anchors, matching on
lowercased text with Croatian locale so that inflected and accented forms are caught. The anchors fall into a few
groups. One is religious instruction, called vjeronauk in Croatian. One is Catholic schools, along with the Catholic
university and seminaries. One is talk of upbringing, values and curriculum. One is the teaching orders and the men
and women in them. The last group is four figures, namely Stepinac, Strossmayer, Stadler and Blessed Marija
Petković. A secondary set of public rituals is kept as a signal but not as
part of the spine. Any post that matches at least one spine anchor, dated between 2021 and 2025, enters the
education strand. The probe only selects a subset. It never changes the corpus wide filter.

Following Nora, an anchor counts as a candidate site of memory to the degree that it scores on four signals. The
first is recurrence, meaning how often it appears, which measures durability rather than a one off. The second is
commemorative peaking, meaning how concentrated its monthly volume is within a stream, and whether its busiest month
returns as the yearly peak across several years, which separates a repeating calendar rite from a single news spike.
The third is emotional weight, measured through the project sentiment and emotion lexicons, CroSentilex for polarity
and lilaHR for eight emotions, on a lemmatised sample, and reported together with how much of the text the lexicon
covers. We treat this third signal as supporting rather than decisive. The fourth is genuine anchoring to the past.
This is not whole document overlap with past reference words such as communism, the year 1991, the Homeland
War or the word for sacrifice. It is windowed overlap, meaning a past word that sits within about 160
characters of the anchor's own mention. This distinguishes memory from mere current affairs that happen to cite the
past.

To check that the measure works we set a positive example and a counter example in advance. Stepinac is expected to
score high, and talk of upbringing and values is expected to score low. If the measure fails to separate them, then
the measure, not the data, is at fault. Genuine anchoring is computed for each anchor on its own, meaning a past
word near that anchor rather than near any anchor at all, so that anchors appearing together in one post cannot lend
each other memory that neither has earned.

Several honesty checks run throughout. We always report the windowed genuine share next to the inflated document
level share, because overlap is not engagement. A first pass revealed a false match. The bare form isusov
matched about 63,000 posts of the possessive Isusov, meaning of Jesus, rather than the Jesuit order, so we
restricted the pattern to the order forms. That removed 32,430 false posts, about 15 in every hundred of the raw
strand, and left the corrected strand of 176,312 posts. All numbers are computed, none typed by hand, the master is
never changed, and the analysis only reads it. Figures use the project's shared visual theme.

## 6. Rezultati / Results

### 6.1 Genuine anchoring to the past, where a single figure stands out

The education strand is large, at 176,312 posts, yet genuine memorial anchoring is concentrated. The full figures
sit in the accompanying candidate table (output/candidate_sites_of_memory.csv), and the pattern is stark. Stepinac's
genuine, windowed anchoring is 0.239, meaning that in almost a quarter of the posts that mention him a past word
sits right beside his name. That is between two and a half and nearly four times the score of any other anchor. The
educational institutions cluster far below, with talk of upbringing and values at 0.098, the religious orders at
0.096, Catholic schools at 0.071 and religious instruction at 0.065. Stepinac also shows the lowest incidental
fraction, meaning that when the past appears near him it is genuinely about him rather than a coincidence. About 57
in every hundred of his document level ties are incidental, against 71 to 90 for the others.

The check on the measure passes cleanly. Stepinac at 0.239 stands well above the counter example, talk of upbringing
and values, at 0.098, a separation of about two and a half times. This held after we corrected an error in the way
the per anchor score was computed, an error caught in internal review, and the separation in fact grew a little
rather than shrinking, which is a sign that it is real rather than an artefact. The first expectation is therefore
borne out.

One method result stands on its own. Across the whole strand, document level overlap with the past runs at 36
in every hundred posts, but windowed genuine anchoring runs at only 11. About 69 in every hundred of the ties are
incidental, a figure that lands almost exactly on a sibling DigiKat study, which found about 68. Had we reported
whole document overlap, we would have overstated memorial engagement by between about two and four times.

### 6.2 A February rhythm

Within each collection stream Stepinac is the spikiest anchor. His monthly volume varies far more than that of any
institution, with a coefficient of variation of 0.76 in the first stream and 0.62 in the second, against roughly
0.44 to 0.48 for religious instruction, Catholic schools and talk of values. More telling than the size of the peak
is its timing. Stepinac's busiest month is February, and February is his yearly peak in three of the four years, a
timing that matches his death anniversary on 10 February 1960, with his beatification on 3 October 1998 as a second
marker. A peak that returns on the same date year after year is the mark of a commemorative rite rather than a news
cycle. The institutions peak in all sorts of months, some tied to the school year and some to the news, and no month
comes back as a regular peak, while the ritual set peaks each August, which fits the summer season of pilgrimages and youth gatherings, themselves calendar events. The
timing signal thus confirms the anchoring signal for Stepinac from an entirely separate direction. The monthly
series and the seasonal profile appear in the accompanying figures (output/figures/stepinac_monthly.png and
output/figures/seasonality_month_of_year.png).

### 6.3 A shared national memory

About 87 in every hundred of the strand's posts come from the web. A map of actors by reach and interaction, which
sorts them into Giants (Divovi), Megaphones (Megafoni), Community Builders (Graditelji zajednica) and Specialists
(Specijalizirani akteri), barely tells the anchors apart. Stepinac leans only slightly toward the Giants. The named outlets tell a sharper story. Joining outlets to the project's proposed
Catholic or secular labels, which cover between about 30 and 44 in every hundred posts and remain the principal
investigator's call, gives a clear pattern (output/tables/confessional_secular_by_entity.csv and
output/tables/top_sources_by_entity.csv).

Stepinac is the most secular shared of all the memory anchors. Among labelled outlets only 62 in every hundred of
his posts come from Catholic sources, against 80 for Catholic schools, 75 for the rituals and 72 for religious
instruction. His leading sources mix Catholic portals, such as the largest Catholic news site and the Zagreb
archdiocese, with a distinctive cluster of secular and nationally minded outlets, among them narod.hr,
hercegbosna.org and dragovoljac.com, and with mainstream press such as Večernji and Slobodna Dalmacija. This is
Nora's point in the data. The site of memory has become national property, argued over and produced well beyond the
Church.

Religious instruction, by contrast, reads as a present tense argument. Alone among all the anchors, its leading
sources include the secular debate forums, with the Croatia community on Reddit contributing 417 posts and forum.hr
another 305, a presence that no other anchor shows. Religious instruction in schools is argued about, not
commemorated. Talk of upbringing and values is the most mainstream and secular of all, spread across the large
national portals, with only 51 in every hundred posts from Catholic sources, which is the profile that sits least
inside the Church. Catholic schools at 80, the rituals at 75 and the orders at 67 remain the most rooted inside the
Church, carried by the Catholic university, the religious orders portal and devotional channels on YouTube and Facebook.
Each anchor thus carries its own audience signature, and those signatures line up along the axis that runs from
memory to present tense argument.

### 6.4 Conflict against trust, a weak but consistent signal

On a lemmatised sample of 5,548 posts, with the CroSentilex lexicon covering about 55 in every hundred tokens,
polarity does not separate the anchors at all. Average polarity sits near minus 0.42 for every anchor, an artefact
of shared news vocabulary and of a lexicon that holds no neutral words, so we do not use polarity and we say so
plainly. The emotion profile from lilaHR is more useful, and it weakly separates three registers. The full profile
sits in the accompanying table and figure (output/tables/affect_by_entity.csv and
output/figures/affect_emotion_profile.png). Stepinac carries the register of conflict and persecution. He shows the
highest anger, fear, sadness and disgust of any anchor, with anger at 0.068, fear at 0.102 and sadness at 0.079, and
the lowest trust, at 0.302. This is the emotional shape of a martyr memory. The institutions carry a register of
trust and pastoral care, with Catholic schools highest on trust at 0.366 and lowest on the conflict emotions, while
the rituals carry a register of joy, highest on that emotion at 0.174. The sizes of these differences are modest and
the coverage is partial, so we treat the third expectation as weakly supported and report the emotion signal as
supporting only.

### 6.5 Convergence

On the signals that tell a site of memory apart, meaning genuine anchoring to the past, commemorative peaking and,
weakly, emotional register, Stepinac scores as the single site of memory, while the educational institutions read,
on those same signals, as a live argument about the values and politics of today. Recurrence does not tell them
apart, since the institutions recur far more than the figure does, which is itself part of the present tense
pattern. The breakdown of the audience confirms the split from a separate direction. No single signal carries the
claim on its own. Their agreement does.

## 7. Interpretacija / Interpretation and discussion

The split is the substance of the paper. The Church understands itself to hand on memory through education, through
catechesis, schools and the formation of the young. The media do something else. They treat those institutions as a
live argument about values, meaning curriculum, parental rights and the place of religion in a secular state, argued
in the present tense and, in the case of religious instruction, contested on secular forums. The work of cultural
memory, the binding of a present identity to a remembered past, is carried instead by a figure. Stepinac shows every
mark of a Nora site of memory, meaning durability, a February commemorative rite, an emotion of persecution and,
above all, a reach beyond the Church into the secular and national public.

Two theoretical points follow. In Assmann's terms the media divide the labour of memory. The institutions occupy
communicative memory, which is present, argued and generationally alive, while the figure occupies cultural memory,
which is canonical, bound to rite and carried by a symbol. Education is the hinge, and in this media space it tilts
toward the communicative side. The second point concerns Stepinac's shared Catholic, secular and national
authorship. This is not noise to be cleaned away. It is the finding. A site of memory is, in Nora's sense, national
property, and the fact that secular and national outlets produce him more than they produce any other anchor, even
though Catholic sources remain the majority, is what makes him a site of memory rather than a private devotion. It also marks the political stakes, since the same figure anchors
both a Church memory of martyrdom and a national memory of communist persecution.

In practical terms the screen, built from four signals, worked as a triage tool. It told a known site of memory apart from a
policy dispute, it surfaced a rare beatified figure, Blessed Marija Petković with only 302 posts, for close reading,
and, through the correction for incidental overlap, it stopped a threefold overstatement of memorial
engagement.

## 8. Ograničenja / Limitations

This is a preliminary analysis based on automatic detection, and every share above is indicative until a coding
check is done. The first and most important next step is to hand code about 150 posts for genuine linkage with a
second coder and to report the level of agreement between them, which turns each share from indicative into measured.
The emotion signal is weak. Polarity is unusable here and the emotion profile is modest and only partly covered, so
the emotion section should be read as supporting only. The past word probe is tuned to the twentieth century rupture
of communism and 1991, so it under detects nineteenth century memory. Strossmayer scores low, at 0.064, even though
he is a textbook site of memory, because his memory runs on a national revival register that the current words miss,
and a second register must be added before a low score is read as an absence of memory. The corpus is posts about
religion inside a space that secular outlets dominate, so the Catholic memory measured here is how the media handle
it, not the Church's own archive. The outlet labels are the principal investigator's proposed and contestable set, covering
between about 30 and 44 in every hundred posts. Finally, the broad anchors bundle several roots together. When they
are pulled apart, the phrase Christian roots barely appears, once in the whole strand, and the word curriculum is
rare, at 1,910 posts, so the broad anchors are driven by very common words such as the word for value, at 60,698,
and the word for upbringing, at 27,455. None of these caveats undo the agreement across signals, but the paper must
state them.

## 9. Zaključak / Conclusion

In Croatian digital media between 2021 and 2025, Catholic education is remembered in the present tense, and the work
of memory is done by a martyr figure. The institutions the Church names as its instruments of transmission, meaning
religious instruction, Catholic schools and the orders, are handled as a live argument about values with little tie
to the past. The figure of Stepinac, by contrast, behaves as a genuine site of memory, on genuine anchoring to the
past, on a February commemorative rhythm, on an emotion of conflict and persecution, and on a shared national
authorship that reaches well beyond the Church. Memory here travels through the martyr, not through the school.

Two contributions travel with the finding. The first is a reusable way of measuring the site of memory through four
signals, checked against a known example and a counter example. The second is a demonstration that overlap is
not memorial engagement, a correction of method that matters on its own. The immediate task is to make the result
firmer. We will add the nineteenth century memory register, hand code the genuine linkage labels, and work in the
Croatian scholarship on the memory of Stepinac and on Catholic media. On that basis the study moves from a working
measure to a close reading of how, in whose words and to what political end, the martyr is remembered where the
school is only debated.
