**Working title:** *Attention without adjustment? Speech, salience, and a two-year price spell in a

## 1. The reframe in one paragraph

v1 is a sector portrait: three responses and an absence, reported in parallel. v2 is a price
rigidity paper in which the church is the case, not the subject. The central problem of the
rigidity literature is that prices are observed and attention is not: a posted price that fails to
move during a shock cannot distinguish a seller who did not notice (sticky information, Mankiw &
Reis; rational inattention, Sims) from one who noticed and faced an adjustment cost (menu costs)
from one who noticed and deliberately held (norms). These map onto different families of model
with different implications for monetary non-neutrality, and the usual tie-breaker is a firm
survey, which is expensive, small, and retrospective (Blinder et al.; Coibion–Gorodnichenko).
This setting offers a second observation channel running in parallel with the price: a dated,
contemporaneous, unprompted record of what the price setter said while its price stood still.
The paper's job is to say precisely what that channel establishes, and its value does not depend
on the strongest reading being true — it depends on being the only design that can distinguish
the readings at all, because everyone else has only the price series.

## 2. The claim ladder (the intellectual core of v2)

The v1 headline — the sector "spoke at the peak and repriced two years later" — supports a weak
claim as it stands and a strong claim only after new coding. State them as a ladder and commit to
climbing only as high as the data allows.

**Rung 0 (established now).** Speech about inflation without adjustment. Weak, because attention
in these models always has an object, and the relevant object is the gap between the current
price and the optimal reset price — not the aggregate price level as a social fact. A bishop
speaking about household hardship is attending to the aggregate as a pastoral matter. It is
entirely coherent to be intensely attentive to inflation as a social phenomenon and completely
inattentive to one's own relative price; that combination is close to the definition of the
inattentive producer (Reis), and Maćkowiak–Wiederholt make the aggregate/idiosyncratic attention
split precise.

**Rung 1 (the target).** Attention to the *own price gap* without adjustment. Established only if
the corpus contains dated statements in which the institution discusses its own costs (heating,
salaries, maintenance, insurance, building repair) or its own revenue position (collections not
covering costs), before the repricing. If such speech exists and repricing still came two years
later, inattention is excluded for the correct object and the strong claim stands.

**Three readings stay open until the coding in §4 is done:**

- **Reading 1 — attention real and correctly located.** Decision makers knew the fees were
  eroding and held anyway. Inattention excluded; the paper is the strong version.
- **Reading 2 — attention to the wrong object.** Inflation was salient as pastoral concern and
  never entered the pricing decision. Inattention is then *confirmed*, not excluded, and the
  honest paper is about the divergence between an institution's public economic concern and its
  own economic behaviour — still publishable, and a clean illustration of how attention to the
  aggregate and attention to the own price gap come apart.
- **Reading 3 — organisational.** Bishops speak; chanceries and parishes price. Attention existed
  somewhere in the institution but had no channel to the unit holding the pen. This is a finding
  about how attention travels inside a hierarchical non-market organisation — a different and
  also publishable paper.

**The fourth problem, acknowledged in the text:** public speech is a strategic act, not a private
cognitive state. Speaking about the cost of living is cheap and gains standing; repricing is
costly and loses it. Own-cost speech partially defuses this — announcing that your heating bill
has doubled is not standing-enhancing cheap talk in the way pastoral concern is — which is a
second reason the own-cost/pastoral split carries the paper.

**How to state the claim:** never write "attention is observed." Write that the sector
demonstrably possessed and publicly discussed information about the shock, then say precisely
which reading that supports and which it does not.

## 3. The four contributions of v2

1. **Joint observation of speech and inaction.** A dated, contemporaneous, unprompted second
   channel alongside the price path. Dated: ordering turns two facts into a lag. Contemporaneous:
   the corpus is a diary, not a memoir — written before anyone knew the ending, immune to the
   retrospective reconstruction that contaminates surveys. Unprompted: volume and timing carry
   information about salience rather than question wording.

2. **The fairness channel watched rather than elicited.** Dual entitlement (Kahneman, Knetsch &
   Thaler): sellers may pass on costs but may not exploit the moment; customer antagonism sits at
   the top of firms' self-reported reasons for not adjusting ever since Blinder, but the
   constraint is always elicited, never observed. A religious institution is the limiting case of
   a seller whose customer relationship is built on non-pecuniary norms, and this one publicly
   committed itself to a fairness position during the shock. The observed pattern — moral speech
   while the shock was salient, repricing after it had passed out of salience — is exactly what a
   fairness-constrained seller does. Claimed as *consistent with, in the setting where the
   mechanism should be maximal*, not as identified: the data can exclude inattention (if Rung 1
   holds) but cannot separate fairness delay from institutional decision cost.

3. **A boundary condition on the frequency–inflation regularity.** From Gagnon (Mexico) and
   Alvarez et al. (high-inflation episodes), the frequency of adjustment rises with inflation.
   This sector's did not, in a service consumed by a large share of the population. The two-year
   spell is a tail observation on a duration distribution estimated almost entirely on
   competitive goods (Dhyne et al. 2006; Nakamura & Steinsson; Klenow & Malin), and it speaks to
   relative price dispersion, where the welfare cost of inflation lives in standard models. The
   comparative statics also discriminate between families: under menu costs a big shock triggers
   adjustment (the selection effect, Golosov–Lucas); under sticky information response is slow
   regardless of size; under a fairness constraint response can be *inverse to salience*, delayed
   precisely when the shock is most visible. The observed pattern is inconsistent with menu-cost
   selection and consistent with the fairness reading; the sticky-information reading is what the
   §4 coding adjudicates.

4. **The measurement template and its warning label.** Media as the residual observation
   instrument for price setters outside both the market and the statistical apparatus with no
   obligation to report (religious bodies, informal activity, clubs, family firms below
   thresholds). The instrument is an **event register, not a price series**: the natural objects
   are timings, hazards, lags, and composition, never levels or elasticities — framed on page
   one. The warning: indicators built by counting documents where two topics co-occur confuse
   topical adjacency with genuine engagement; here the overstatement is roughly tenfold
   (1 450 → 520), and when the true engagement rate is low the false-positive rate dominates the
   count entirely. Precision beats recall exactly where text indicators are most tempting — rare
   topics with no official measurement. This belongs in the abstract, not an appendix.
   (Gentzkow–Kelly–Taddy 2019; Grimmer–Stewart 2013; Baker–Bloom–Davis as the index template.)

**Demoted to discussion:** the rarity of the distributional claim, reframed as a fact about the
supply of economic narratives (Larsen–Thorsrud; D'Acunto–Weber; Shiller): a large institution
with an explicit doctrinal commitment to distributional concern almost never voiced it, so
narrative supply in the public sphere is thinner than institutional commitments predict. One
subsection, no longer a co-headline.

## 4. New empirical work (what actually has to be done)

Nothing below collects new data. W1 and W2 recode material already coded, W3 is an analytic
argument from quantities already in the tables, and W4 re-describes an existing series.

**W1 — Recode speech content for the attention object.** This is the decisive addition and it
uses material already coded. Split the public-voice material and the sector-as-economic-actor
category (179 posts, which already contains church finances and clergy pay) into:
(a) **own-cost / own-revenue speech** — the institution discussing its heating, salaries,
maintenance, insurance, repairs, collections, budget; and
(b) **household-hardship / pastoral speech** — the cost of living as other people's burden.
Produce two dated monthly series and report their peaks separately; if they peak at different
dates, that difference is itself a result about what the institution was attending to when.
The outcome of W1 selects the reading in §2 and therefore the final frame. Protocol addendum to
Appendix B; same three-annotator majority design; add the split to the recoding validation.

**W2 — Unit matching.** Tag the institutional unit in each coded post (parish, diocese, order,
Caritas, bishops' conference, Vatican). A diocese that spoke about its own costs and later
repriced links attention and inaction inside one decision-making body — far stronger than an
aggregate coincidence of national speech and national repricing. Even a handful of matched pairs
upgrades the evidence class; their absence, with speech and pricing located in different units,
is Reading 3 and is reported as such. Disclosure: unit-level detail that identifies sources goes
to `output/private/` per `R/check_disclosure.R`; the paper reports aggregate patterns.

**W3 — Detection-lag and reporting-endogeneity argument, from internal evidence only.** No new
data is collected; the argument is analytic and uses what the corpus already shows. Coverage is
the product of an event rate and a reporting propensity, and the propensity is endogenous to
inflation, peaking with the shock. Both directions are stated explicitly. The speech-at-peak
finding is partly mechanical and is claimed modestly. The repricing-two-years-later finding is
*conservative*, because the reporting filter worked against detection once attention had faded —
the bias runs against the headline result, which is the strongest available statement about it.
The differential-newsworthiness threat (a bishop's statement is cheap to report, a parish price
list change is not, so the measured lag could mix a behavioural lag with a detection lag) is
answered from inside the corpus on three grounds. First, capability: the same instrument,
method A, detected repricing coverage already in 2022 (46 posts) and 2023 (34 posts), so the
instrument demonstrably could see church repricing during the shock window; what peaks in 2024
is not the arrival of detection ability. Second, composition: the shift from speech towards
repricing inside method A alone (30.3 → 47.9 per cent of the two categories across 2022–2023,
93.8 per cent of A's 2024 material) is a within-instrument statement that a late-arriving
detection channel cannot produce. Third, incentive: the price-of-services category is carried
almost entirely by secular outlets (189 of 194 posts) in an adversarial register, and an outlet
with no stake in the sector had the *strongest* incentive to report a church price rise
precisely when inflation was front-page news — so detection propensity for repricing was, if
anything, highest during the shock, which again biases against finding a late peak. Given no
external validation set, the headline is stated as the timing of *coverage of repricing*, with
these three grounds offered for reading it as behaviour; recovering documented fee schedules
from diocesan sources is named as the first extension and held in reserve for referees.

**W4 (optional) — Event-process description.** Describe repricing timing as a dated event
process: cumulative price-level gap at first adjustment (~25 per cent), spell length, and a
descriptive hazard framing. No structural estimation; the point is to make the "hazards and lags,
not levels" framing concrete.

## 5. Structure of the v2 manuscript

1. **Introduction** — the two-baker problem (a price that does not move cannot distinguish the
   seller who did not notice from the one who noticed and held); the second channel; the claim
   ladder stated honestly; the four contributions.
2. **Why the tie matters** — the three model families, their different implications for
   non-neutrality, the selection effect, why surveys are the usual escape and what they cannot
   date. (Absorbs v1 §2.1 and expands it.)
3. **The sector as the limiting case** — no competitor, no indexation, maximal non-pecuniary
   customer relationship, public fairness commitment; nonprofit pricing (Steinberg; Hansmann;
   Jackson 2014 on credit unions as the one direct empirical neighbour); doctrine in one
   paragraph as the source of the public commitment, not as a co-equal research question.
4. **Data and instrument** — corpus, two-method seam (unchanged), event-register framing, the
   co-occurrence arithmetic, coding and validation (unchanged), W1/W2 additions, the W3
   detection argument.
5. **Results** — instrument check (unchanged); the timing result within-method (unchanged); the
   attention-object split (new, decides the frame); unit-matched cases (new); the distributional
   absence as narrative supply (demoted).
6. **Discussion** — which reading survived, stated exactly; fairness as the preferred consistent
   mechanism with its identification limits; the boundary condition; the template.

## 8. Risks and honest limits

- **The coding may select Reading 2.** Then the frame is rewritten to the divergence paper —
  planned for above, not a failure mode. The proposal's branch structure exists so that no
  outcome of W1 strands the manuscript.
- **Speech is strategic.** Acknowledged in text; own-cost speech is the partial answer; no claim
  that communication certifies cognition.
- **LLM-only coding.** Same-family reliability limitation carries over from v1 verbatim; the W1
  split must be included in the recoding validation.
- **Detection lag.** With no external validation set in scope, the headline is stated as the
  timing of coverage of repricing, defended as behaviour on the three internal grounds of W3
  (capability shown in 2022–2023, within-instrument composition shift, adversarial secular
  detection incentive strongest during the shock). A referee can still ask for documented fee
  schedules; the reply is that the extension is named in the paper and that every internal bias
  identified runs against the headline. Diocesan fee-schedule recovery stays first on the
  extensions list for a revision round, not in this submission's scope.
- **Fairness vs institutional decision cost.** Not separable even in the best case; the paper
  claims consistency in the maximal setting, and says so.
