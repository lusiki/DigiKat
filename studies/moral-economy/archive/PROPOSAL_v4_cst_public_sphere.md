# Part 0 — The article in 90 seconds

The Catholic Church has been writing about the economy since 1891 — on work, wages, property and poverty.
That body of thought is **Catholic Social Teaching (CST)**. We ask whether it is actually used when the
Church appears in public economic argument.

Across five years of Croatian digital media we identified **108,966** posts where religion and economics
genuinely meet. Explicit doctrine appears in **1,198 — about 1 in 91**.

The scarcity has a **shape**, and the shape runs backwards to the tradition. Doctrine is invoked most on
**climate and energy (7.15%)** — the Church's newest economic subject, barely present in Catholic teaching
before *Laudato si'* (2015) — and least on the labour-capital subjects the tradition was built for
(**business 0.72%, wages 0.95%, housing 0.34%**).

**Thesis.** Doctrinal uptake follows **agenda convergence**, not doctrinal depth: teaching becomes publicly
sayable where a secular argument is already running, and falls silent where the Church would have to speak
on its own authority. The Church's economic voice is loudest exactly where it is least distinctive.

**Working titles**
1. *Charity for the Poor, Justice for the Climate: Catholic Social Teaching in the Croatian Digital Public Sphere*
2. *The Silent Tradition: Where the Church Speaks — and Does Not — About the Economy*
3. *Doctrine Follows the Agenda*

# Part I — The argument

## I.1 The puzzle
A 130-year moral tradition about economic life exists. Does anyone use it? We can answer at the scale of a
national media ecosystem rather than by reading a handful of documents.

## I.2 The finding — invocation rate by economic domain

Rate = core posts ÷ religion-linked posts in that domain. Wilson 95% CI.

| domain | linked | core | rate | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **green_energy** | 3,425 | 245 | **7.15%** | 6.34–8.07 |
| macro_aggregates | 7,090 | 156 | 2.20% | 1.88–2.57 |
| poverty_social | 38,232 | 727 | 1.90% | 1.77–2.04 |
| taxes_fiscal | 23,375 | 346 | 1.48% | 1.33–1.64 |
| demography_econ | 1,288 | 17 | 1.32% | 0.83–2.10 |
| unemployment | 5,849 | 74 | 1.27% | 1.01–1.59 |
| wages_income | 13,724 | 130 | 0.95% | 0.80–1.12 |
| business_comp | 31,416 | 227 | 0.72% | 0.63–0.82 |
| inflation_prices | 1,607 | 6 | 0.37% | 0.17–0.81 |
| housing | 6,106 | 21 | 0.34% | 0.23–0.53 |
| euro_changeover | 407 | 0 | 0.00% | 0.00–0.94 |

Green energy is a clean outlier; its interval does not approach any other domain's.
*Counts are domain-mentions; posts average 1.63 domains.*

## I.3 It survives every robustness check
Green energy ranks **first in every subset**, and the gap **widens** when concentration is removed:

| subset | green | next best | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| all | 7.15% | 2.20% | 3.3× |
| excluding hkm.hr (largest outlet, 32%) | 6.34% | 1.50% | 4.2× |
| excluding top 3 outlets | 5.95% | 1.35% | 4.4× |
| **secular outlets only** | 6.91% | 1.56% | 4.4× |
| confessional outlets only | 19.22% | 16.70% | 1.15× |

## I.4 The refinement that carries the "public sphere" claim
The last two rows are the most interesting result in the study:

- **Confessional media** cite doctrine across many topics fairly evenly (climate 19.2%, macro 16.7%).
- **Secular media** let doctrine through almost *only* on climate (6.9% vs 1.6% next).

**The topical selection happens at the boundary between confessional and secular media, not inside the
Church's own discourse.** This is the paper's sharpest contribution to the public-religion literature.

## I.5 Why recency does not explain it
The obvious objection: Francis was pope for most of the period. But pure recency predicts *uniform* era
shares across topics. Observed (row % of core posts):

| domain | classical (1891–1991) | francis |
|---|---|---|
| green_energy | **0.8%** | 88.2% |
| housing | 14.3% | 38.1% |
| wages_income | 13.1% | 44.6% |
| business_comp | 11.5% | 40.5% |
| poverty_social | 5.5% | 57.4% |

Classical share is 14–18× higher in labour-capital domains than in climate. Recency is **insufficient** —
but Francis dominates everywhere, so recency is clearly *also* operating. Report **both mechanisms with
separable magnitudes**; do not declare a winner.

## I.6 Two registers
Log-odds keyness (Monroe, Colaresi & Quinn 2008) against 2,000 matched non-core posts, identical ±220
windows both sides:

- **Over-represented:** *papa, socijalan, franjo, klimatski, promjena, enciklika, nauk, moralan, okoliš, ekološki*
- **Under-represented:** *župa, misa, blagoslov, svećenik, molitva, križ, pomoć, posvetiti*

Doctrinal discourse is **papal, systemic, climate-inflected**; ordinary religious-economic discourse is
**parochial, liturgical, charitable**. Unsupervised, so not an artefact of our categories.

## I.7 The sting — poverty
Poverty is the largest contact zone (**38,232** linked posts, 37.3% of core domain-mentions). CST's own
principle for it, *opcija za siromašne*, appears **29 times corpus-wide**. The Church discusses the poor
constantly as people to help, almost never as evidence of an unjust arrangement. Thesis in miniature; best
single case study.

## I.8 Contribution
1. **A census, not a sample** — complete 710,307-post corpus; no sampling error.
2. **A non-English case with auditable measurement** — every rule versioned, every matched post inspectable.
3. **A structured absence** — the *shape* of the absence discriminates between explanations.

# Part II — Status of every claim

**Epistemic rule for this study:** most quantities come from a **census of the whole population**, so there
is no sampling error and **no null-hypothesis test is appropriate**. The risks are *measurement validity*
and *interpretation*. Do not attach p-values to census proportions.

| Claim | Evidence | Status | What would kill it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Doctrine is rare — 1.10%, ceiling 10.5% | census | **Established** | Tier-1 precision audit below ~0.6 |
| Rate varies ~21× across domains; climate is the outlier | census + 5 robustness subsets | **Established** | — |
| Recency is insufficient | era × domain non-uniformity | **Established** | uniform era shares |
| Topical selection happens at the confessional/secular boundary | census, post-labelling | **Established** | — |
| Doctrine is enclosed in confessional media | 70.4% vs 43.3% after labelling | **Established** (was blocked; now resolved) | — |
| Two registers (justice vs charity) | keyness only | **Suggestive — one method** | hand coding disagrees |
| Poverty handled as charity, ecology as justice | 29 mentions + 12 coded climate posts | **Suggestive — thin** | poverty codes predominantly justice |
| *Why* — agenda convergence | none | **Interpretation, untested** | — |

**Explicitly dropped:** we expected secular outlets to take up climate-linked doctrine more readily than
labour-linked doctrine. **They do not** — 21.2% secular for Francis-era vs 26.1% for classical, intervals
heavily overlapping. Report the null.

**Deliberately not pursued:** a blind magisterial "doctrinal depth" ranking (PI decision, 2026-08-04). The
consequence is that the depth comparison is **framing in the discussion, not a measured variable**. That
*Rerum Novarum* is about labour and *Laudato si'* about ecology is the documentary record and needs no
validation. Do **not** write a correlation between depth and invocation — there is no depth variable.

# Part IV — The population, defined exactly

```
710,307   posts, Croatian/Bosnian digital media 2021–2026 (≥2 distinct religious terms)
108,966   religion-linked economic posts (11 domains × religion within ±220 chars)
  1,198   + Tier-1 doctrine within ±220 chars of the economic term   ← THE ANALYSIS POPULATION
```

Three conditions, all required (`cst_core.R`):
1. Tier-1 CST vocabulary — 16 magisterial titles + coinages with no ordinary Croatian sense
   (*socijalni nauk*, *supsidijarnost*, *integralna ekologija*, *opcija za siromašne*);
2. membership of the religion-linked economic layer (`stageA_candidates.rds`);
3. the CST term within ±220 characters of an economic term.

**Tier 2** (*solidarnost*, *opće dobro*, *dostojanstvo rada*, *socijalna pravda*) is ordinary political
vocabulary. It **bounds** the count from above (ceiling 10.5%) and must **never** be used to establish
presence — or to define adjacency (see Part VII).

Descriptive profile of the population: **97.6% web**; median doctrine↔economy gap **75 characters**;
era: francis 644, marker_only 309, mixed 100, classical 81, conciliar 56, benedict 8.

# Part VII — Traps

1. **Never let Tier 2 define membership or adjacency.** It bounds; it does not measure. This has already
   caused one wrong population (1,552 instead of 1,198).
2. **`cst_build_core()` aborts if the count ≠ 1,198.** That guard is deliberate. If you intend to change the
   population, pass `expect = <new>` *explicitly* and update Part IV, the profile and the proposal together.
3. **No p-values on census proportions.** You have the population, not a sample.
4. **No temporal claims.** Ever. The streams are time-segregated by construction.
5. **hkm.hr is 32% of the population.** Every estimate needs a leave-one-out check; cluster SEs by outlet.
6. **Disclosure.** `output/private/**` never gets committed, published, or turned into a web artifact — it
   carries row-level text, URLs and outlet identities.
7. **Plan-gated files:** `resources/dictionaries/**`, `resources/lexicons/**`, `R/religious_terms.R`, the
   master, `R/03_aggregate.R --apply`, full `quarto render`.
8. **LLM annotators.** The 555-item set is three blind LLM passes with majority adjudication. The codebook
   promises a **human double-coded slice** on Axes 4 and 6 and there is no evidence it was run. This is
   **blocking for a sociology-of-religion venue** if any coded claim enters the paper.
9. **Run R and Quarto from the repo root.** The clone sits inside Dropbox — pause sync for long runs
   (`CLAUDE.local.md`).
10. **`viewer_lib.R` had a JS bug** (a no-filter preset emitted `all:{:''}`, killing the whole script and
    silently disabling every filter on every viewer page). Fixed 2026-08-04. If you add a preset with no
    filters, verify the page's `<script>` still parses (`node --check`).
