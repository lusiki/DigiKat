# Part 0 — The article in 90 seconds

The Catholic Church has been writing about economic life since 1891 — about work, wages, property and
poverty. That body of thought is **Catholic Social Teaching (CST)**. Thirty-two years ago this journal
opened its first issue by asking what CST could contribute to Croatian social policy (Valković, 1994.).
That was a normative question, asked before it could be answered empirically. It now can be.

Across five years of Croatian digital media we identified **108 966** posts where religion and economics
genuinely meet. Explicit doctrine appears in **1 198 — about 1 in 91**.

The scarcity has a **shape**, and the shape runs backwards to the tradition. Doctrine is invoked most on
**climate and energy (7,15%)** — the Church's newest economic subject, barely present in Catholic teaching
before *Laudato si'* (2015) — and least on the labour-and-livelihood subjects the tradition was built for
(**business 0,72%, wages 0,95%, housing 0,34%, prices 0,37%, the euro changeover 0,00%**).

Poverty is the largest contact zone in the entire corpus — **38 232** linked posts, 37,3% of all doctrinal
domain-mentions. Yet CST's own principle for exactly this, *opcija za siromašne* (the preferential option
for the poor), appears **29 times in 710 307 posts**.

**Thesis.** The Church discusses the poor constantly, as people to be helped, and almost never as evidence
of an unjust arrangement. Where it does deploy systemic-justice language, it does so on climate — where a
secular argument was already running. Doctrinal uptake follows **agenda convergence**, not doctrinal depth:
teaching becomes publicly sayable where a secular argument already exists, and falls silent where the
Church would have to speak on its own authority. **The consequence for social policy is that Croatia's
largest non-state welfare actor supplies the public sphere with a vocabulary of charity, not of
entitlement.**

## I.4 The refinement that carries the public-sphere claim

- **Confessional media** cite doctrine across many topics fairly evenly — climate 22,31%, macro 15,96%,
  a ratio of only 1,40× and the *one* subset in the whole programme where the intervals overlap.
- **Secular media** let doctrine through almost *only* on climate — 7,52% vs 1,98% next (3,79×), and
  5,16% vs 1,18% (4,36×) under the widest secular definition.

**The topical selection happens at the boundary between confessional and secular media, not inside the
Church's own discourse.** The Church is not choosing to talk only about climate; the secular press is
choosing to relay only the climate part.

## I.5 Why recency does not explain it

The obvious objection: Francis was pope for most of the period. But pure recency predicts *uniform* era
shares across topics. Observed (row % of doctrinal posts; **verified against `cst_core_domain_era.csv`**):

| domain | classical (1891–1991) | francis |
|---|---|---|
| green_energy | **0,8%** | 88,2% |
| business_comp | 11,5% | 40,5% |
| wages_income | 13,1% | 44,6% |
| housing | 14,3% | 38,1% |
| poverty_social | 5,5% | 57,4% |

Classical share is 14–18× higher in labour-and-livelihood domains than in climate. Recency is
**insufficient** — but Francis dominates everywhere, so recency is clearly *also* operating.
**Report both mechanisms with separable magnitudes; do not declare a winner.**

## I.6 The sting — poverty

Poverty is the largest contact zone: **38 232** linked posts, 37,3% of all doctrinal domain-mentions.
CST's own principle for it, *opcija za siromašne*, appears **29 times corpus-wide** — 1,46% of doctrinal
term occurrences, behind eleven encyclical titles. The Church discusses the poor constantly as people to
help, almost never as evidence of an unjust arrangement.

**This is the paper's centre of gravity for RSP.** It is the section that converts a religion finding into
a social-policy finding, and it is where the discussion in §7 must land.

## I.7 What we are NOT claiming (put this in the paper, not just here)

- **No causal claim.** No individual-level outcomes, no exogenous variation. We measure the supply of
  moral vocabulary; we do not estimate its effect on attitudes, votes or policy.
- **No temporal claim.** The two collection streams are time-segregated by construction; year-over-year
  movement is a collection artefact. Never plot the doctrinal count over time.
- **No claim about what the Church privately believes.** We observe published media text only.
- **No claim about doctrinal depth as a measured variable.** That *Rerum Novarum* is about labour and
  *Laudato si'* about ecology is the documentary record, used as framing, never as a correlate.

## I.8 Contribution

1. **A census, not a sample** — the complete corpus; no sampling error in the headline quantities.
2. **A non-English case with auditable measurement** — every rule versioned, every matched post inspectable.
3. **A structured absence** — the *shape* of the absence discriminates between explanations.
4. **A social-policy payoff** — it names the vocabulary available to Croatian poverty debate, reopening a
   question this journal asked in 1994 and left unanswered.

# Part V — THE ROBUSTNESS PROGRAMME

Each item states the **threat**, the **test**, the **pass criterion**, and **what a failure costs**.

## V.1 Measurement validity of the numerator

### R1 — Tier-1 precision audit · **BLOCKING for C1**
- **Threat.** "The regex found 1 198" is not "1 198 posts invoke the tradition." A title may be a
  bibliographic listing, a book advert, a conference programme, a byline.
- **Test.** Read ~150 cards in `output/private/cst_viewer.html`, stratified by era × domain tier using the
  allocation table already computed in `CST_CORE_PROFILE.md` §6 and the study seed (`ME_SEED = 20260804`).
  Record: genuine invocation / mere mention / false match. **Stratify by outlet too**, or the largest
  confessional portal (32,0%) dominates the sample.
- **Pass.** Precision ≥ 0,80 → report the rate as measured. 0,60–0,80 → report both raw and
  precision-adjusted. < 0,60 → the headline number cannot be published as-is.
- **Cost of failure.** The 1,10% headline, and with it the paper.

### R2 — Domain-regex precision, numerator side
- **Threat.** A doctrinal post may be assigned to the wrong economic domain, moving mass between rows of
  the key table.
- **Test.** In the same 150-card audit, also record whether the matched economic term is a genuine economic
  reference in context. Report per-domain.
- **Pass.** No domain below 0,70. Green energy specifically ≥ 0,80, since the whole argument turns on it.
- **Cost.** The gradient's ordering.

### R3 — The metaphor problem
- **Threat.** `inflation_prices` already carries 7 metaphor flags at Stage A ("inflacija titula",
  "inflacija riječi"). Metaphorical economic language inflates denominators and can create spurious nulls.
- **Test.** Read all 6 doctrinal inflation posts (trivially cheap — n = 6) and a 50-post sample of the
  1 607 linked ones.
- **Pass.** Metaphor share of the denominator < 0,10, or report the corrected rate.
- **Cost.** The "silence on prices" line, which is rhetorically load-bearing in §0 and §4.

## V.2 Measurement validity of the DENOMINATOR — the largest unaddressed risk

### R4 — Domain-varying linkage precision · **BLOCKING · now known to be a LIVE threat, not a hypothetical one**

> **STATUS CHANGED 2026-08-04 (second check pass).** This item previously said linkage precision "has
> never been coded". **That was wrong.** It *was* coded: `08_build_gold_sheet.R` → `09b_iaa_validate.R`
> produced an adjudicated 555-item gold set with an `ax1_link_genuine` axis (Fleiss κ = **0,941**), and
> `PAPER_v1.md` §4.1 already reports the pooled result. What had never been produced is the **per-domain**
> breakdown — which is the quantity this paper's gradient depends on. `18_gold_reanalysis.R` now produces
> it, and **the news is bad.**

**What we now know.**

1. **Pooled genuine-link rate is 34,6%** (192 of 555). Roughly two thirds of the "religion meets economics"
   layer is not a genuine engagement — homonyms (*zadužen za* = "responsible for", not "indebted";
   *primanje* of a sacrament rather than of income), churches appearing as venues or street names, and
   religious articles with an incidental economic word.
2. **It varies by more than an order of magnitude across domains** — from **3,1%** (macro_aggregates,
   1 of 32) to **53,1%** (housing, 17 of 32) in the coded set. χ²(10) = 71,6, p ≈ 2×10⁻¹¹.
3. **Naively precision-adjusting the gradient destroys it.** Dividing each rate by its domain's genuine
   rate moves climate from 1st (7,15%) to 3rd (20,3%), behind macro_aggregates (71,0%) and business
   (12,9%). Top domain raw = `green_energy`; top domain adjusted = `macro_aggregates` or `wages_income`
   depending on which precision estimate is used.

**Why this is NOT yet fatal — and why it is not yet survivable either.**

The 555 is a **stratified set built for other purposes** (`domain_core` 219, `register_cell` 119,
`recall_gap` 88, `poverty_split` 60, `precision_gap` 41, `random_linked` 28). Only `random_linked` is an
unbiased draw from the linked layer, and at **n = 28** — two posts for climate, four for business — it
cannot estimate anything. The per-domain rates above therefore describe *the coded sample*, not the layer.
The adjusted rates are also arithmetically unstable: dividing by 0,031 amplifies a single coding decision
into a 71% rate, and the numerator is not precision-adjusted in the same operation.

**So the honest position is: the gradient may or may not survive, and the existing data cannot say which.**

**Test (the real one, and it has to be done).** Draw a **fresh stratified random sample from the linked
layer, by domain** — target ≥ 60 posts per domain, ~660 total, seeded with `ME_SEED` — and code the single
axis `ax1_link_genuine` against the existing codebook definition. Then recompute the gradient as
`doctrinal ÷ (linked × precision_domain)` with intervals propagated from both terms.

**Pass.** Green first with a disjoint interval after adjustment.
**Cost of failure.** The invocation-rate gradient — the paper's current headline — cannot be published.
See Part V.7 for what the paper becomes in that case, which is not nothing.

### R5 — Near-duplicate and syndicated content · **new, and confirmed present**
- **Threat.** URL-level dedup (`canonicalize_url()`) removes identical URLs. It does **not** remove the same
  article text republished at different URLs. In a population of 1 198, a wire story carried by 44 outlets
  is 3,7% of the population.
- **Evidence it is real.** Across 2 714 occurrences of *"Laudato si"* in the matched set there are 1 397
  distinct 90-character contexts; the most repeated identical context recurs **44 times**. That is
  syndication, not coincidence.
- **Test.** Normalise text (lowercase, strip punctuation and whitespace), shingle at k = 8, cluster by
  Jaccard ≥ 0,9 (or hash the first 400 normalised characters as a cheap first pass). Report the
  duplicate-cluster size distribution for both the 1 198 and the 108 966.
- **Pass.** Recompute the domain gradient on a **de-duplicated** population, one post per cluster. Report
  both. Green must remain first.
- **Cost.** Every count in the paper shifts. Better to find this now than in review.

### R6 — Boilerplate and self-referential outlet names

## V.4 Confounds in the headline gradient

### R11 — Foreign-news contamination · **the one confound that runs WITH the finding**
- **Threat.** *Laudato si'* is a global document, and papal climate statements are international news.
  If climate-linked doctrinal posts are disproportionately foreign wire coverage of Vatican events, the
  "green outlier" is partly a story about **what gets syndicated from Rome**, not about Croatian public
  argument.
- **Evidence it is live.** `stageA_domain_stats.csv` carries `foreign_hint_rate` per domain:
  green 0,246 vs poverty 0,109 — climate's denominator is **2,3× more foreign-flagged** than poverty's.
  Euro changeover (0,409) and inflation (0,281) are higher still.
- **Test.** Recompute the full gradient on domestic-only posts (`foreign_hint == 0`). Report as a row in
  the I.3 robustness table.
- **Pass.** Green remains first with a disjoint interval.
- **Cost of failure.** Not fatal, but it changes the paper's claim from "Croatian public argument" to
  "Croatian relay of Vatican communication" — which is still publishable and arguably more interesting,
  but must be stated honestly and would need the title changed.

### R12 — Stream composition
- **Threat.** The two collection streams have different linkage rates per domain
  (e.g. taxes 0,510 in 2021–24 vs 0,309 in 2024–26). Since the streams are time-segregated, any domain
  whose linkage differs across streams has a composition-driven rate.
- **Test.** Recompute the gradient **within each stream separately**. Both must give green first.
- **Pass.** Ordering stable within stream.
- **Note.** This is *not* a temporal analysis and must not be presented as one. It is a composition check.
  Say so explicitly in the caption or a referee will read it as a trend.

### R13 — Concentration and clustering
- **Threat.** The largest outlet is 32,0% of the population; the top 3 are 41,7%. Unweighted description
  of "Croatian Catholic economic discourse" is substantially a description of one newsroom's house style.
- **Test.** (i) leave-one-outlet-out across the top 10 (R6); (ii) cluster-robust intervals by outlet on any
  comparison between domains or outlet types; (iii) an outlet-weighted variant where each outlet
  contributes equally, reported alongside the raw figure.
- **Pass.** Green first in all variants; the confessional/secular contrast survives clustering.

## V.7 Contingency — the paper if R4 goes against us

**Do not treat this as a disaster plan; treat it as the likelier plan.** A fresh precision sample may well
show that the invocation-rate gradient is substantially a denominator artefact. The study is not lost if it
does, because a second, independent finding is already hand-coded and is **immune to the denominator
problem** — it conditions on genuine links only.

`18_gold_reanalysis.R`, on the 192 adjudicated genuine links (3-way register, Cohen/Fleiss κ = **0,844**):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| poverty_social | 85 | 7,1% | 28,2% | **21,2%** | 43,5% |
| green_energy | 12 | 8,3% | 0,0% | **91,7%** | 0,0% |
| unemployment | 13 | 30,8% | 15,4% | 53,8% | 0,0% |
| housing | 17 | 58,8% | 35,3% | 5,9% | 0,0% |
| wages_income | 17 | 52,9% | 29,4% | 11,8% | 5,9% |
| inflation_prices | 11 | **100,0%** | 0,0% | 0,0% | 0,0% |
| euro_changeover | 12 | 83,3% | 16,7% | 0,0% | 0,0% |

**This is the paper's title, hand-coded: charity (plus devotional framing) for the poor, justice for the
climate, and the Church-as-institution for everything else.** Poverty carries the *lowest* justice share
of any domain with meaningful n except housing; climate carries 91,7%.

**The fallback paper** therefore keeps §1, §2, §3, §6 and §7 intact and replaces §4–§5 with the register
contrast as the primary result. It becomes a study of **how** religion engages the economy rather than
**how often** — which is arguably the better social-policy paper anyway, because "in what register is
poverty discussed" bears on welfare legitimation more directly than "how often is an encyclical cited".

**Costs of the fallback, stated honestly:**
- n is small per domain (2–85) and the intervals are wide. Only poverty (85) and the poverty-vs-rest
  contrast are comfortably powered.
- The coders are three blind LLM passes, not humans (`PAPER_v1.md` §6.1). The κ figures are *inter-pass*
  reliability. **A human double-coded slice becomes genuinely blocking in this version**, because the
  coded data is no longer a supporting check — it is the result. This reverses PI decision R8(a).
- It overlaps `PAPER_v1.md` §4.4. The two papers must be separated deliberately, not accidentally.

## XII.1 R4 — the denominator was the problem it was feared to be, and the finding survives anyway

| Quantity | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| sample | 660 (rid, domain) pairs, 60/domain, random within domain, 555 gold rids excluded | `19_r4_linkage_sample.R` |
| genuine-link rate, layer-weighted | **39,5%** (codebook axis) · **26,8%** (strict: economic referent required) | `r4_linkage_precision.csv` |
| range across domains | **21,7% – 63,3%**, χ²(10) = 51,1 | same |
| corrected headline | **3,73% — about 1 in 27** genuine contacts (5,48%, 1 in 18, strict) | `20_r4_recompute.R` §C |
| corrected gradient, top | green_energy **14,80% [11,80; 19,86]** vs macro 7,77% | `cst_gradient_adjusted.csv` |
| **P(green ranks first after correction)** | **0,987** codebook · **0,937** strict | `20_r4_recompute.R` §B |
| ratio to runner-up | 3,25× raw → **1,91×** corrected (1,73× strict) | same |

**Read this correctly, because it cuts both ways.**
- **C2 SURVIVES and can be upgraded from Contingent to Established-with-correction.** The threat was
  real and it was tested on a sample drawn for the purpose; green still ranks first.
- **C1 DOES NOT survive as written and must be rewritten.** "1,10%, about 1 in 91" divides by a
  denominator that is ~60% not a religion–economy encounter. The publishable number is **1 in 27**.
- **The gap narrows a lot.** 3,25× → 1,91×. The reason is that `poverty_social` has the *highest*
  genuine-link rate of any domain (63,3%) while taxes and business are homonym-riddled, so the
  correction penalises exactly climate's nearest rivals. Any draft that keeps "twenty-one-fold" as a
  headline is now wrong.
- The 555-item gold set's per-domain rates (3,1%–53,1%) were **an artefact of its stratification**.
  `18_gold_reanalysis.R`'s alarming ordering does not reproduce on a random draw. Do not cite it.
