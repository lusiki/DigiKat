Each belongs to a different exact title/body group. Near-identical quotations can recur across those groups; the sample is intentionally not an estimate of prevalence.

Three show a clearly misplaced interpretive basis; the fourth has uncertain attribution and is not accepted as a confirmed Christian principle application. Five archived records share the four flagged exact contexts.

## Topic-label decisions

The ten original labels are retained for comparability. They are vocabulary summaries rather than gold content classes. Typical cases frequently repeat the same quote even after exact full-text grouping. Boundary cases cross editorial labels: domestic policy can appear under international relations; a church election appeal can appear in an electoral/conservative component. Full results and cautions are in `topic_content_review.csv`.

Do not translate the 50% assigned to t01-t04 into a verified prevalence of the identity function. Do not interpret the political-affiliation component as support: its typical cases include explicit refusal of affiliation while respecting the principles.

## Qualitative principles and applications

Positive examples were read as linked propositions, not keyword co-presence:

- Worker dignity/solidarity: May 2026 appeal explicitly connects those values with work, productivity and economic development. This is an argument in the statement, not evidence of policy success.
- Common good/person dignity: April 2024 criticism of party benefit and ideological exclusion links principles to political participation, freedom of expression and merit-based employment.
- Subsidiarity: February 2021 explanatory conversation links the named principle to lower and supranational levels of government. It is the speaker's interpretation; the report does not certify doctrinal correctness or equate every transfer of authority with subsidiarity.
- Conscience: April 2022 statement invokes Christian-democratic values to justify opposition to prohibiting conscientious objection. It is a claimed political application, not a legal finding or normative endorsement.
- European solidarity: June 2024 campaign argument connects solidarity to the development of a member country. It does not establish an economic causal effect.

Negative/boundary distinctions: listing solidarity is not a specific application; dignity attached to a title or a particular collective is not automatically human dignity; a named person is not necessarily a speaker; a recommended article is not the main article's argument; criticism of a party need not reject Christian democracy; a self-identified Christian democrat may reject that party.

No automatic prevalence of these interpretive labels is released. No matrix of confirmed applications, stance ranking, geographical prevalence, speaker percentages, or reliability metric is fabricated.

## Selected lexical relations

Domoljublje/narodnjaštvo/državotvornost appears as a repeated identity formula and in criticism of inconsistency. Democracy/conservatism co-occurrence includes explicit differentiation. Europe/solidarity has substantive examples but also broad lists and organizational names. HDZ/conscience includes both conscientious objection and an appeal to vote according to conscience. The latter must not be collapsed into one policy position.

All five selected pairs remain positive under the adjacent-sentence, distinct-sentence and short-sentence controls. Raw sentence forms are sensitive to punctuation; the word-sequence control is separately provided. Removing a single most widespread identical sentence's groups does not remove all paraphrases of the same statement.
